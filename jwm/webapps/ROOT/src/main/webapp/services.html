RV Winterizing

Winterizing? We're in Texas... not Wisconsin... Correct, but think back to a few recent winters with temperatures remaining below freezing for several days. Harse winter days don't last all that long here. But they can easily do as much costly damage to your RV plumbing in those few days as they would in Wisconsin. If the weather forecast says drip your pipes at home, your RV needs attention. Winterizing consists of draining and flushing all of the water from all pipes, water heater, fresh water tank, and low point drains. After flushing, the pipes are filled with food-safe RV anti-freeze. De-winterizing reverses the process by flushing out the antifreeze and refilling with fresh water.

RV Inspections Information and Pricing

An RV is a major purchase. Just as you'd have a home inspected before buying it, you can have an independent inspector look over the RV before you sign. That applies whether you're buying a used RV or taking delivery of a brand-new RV.

On a used RV, the inspection can help you spot wear, damage, or systems that aren't working as expected. On a new RV, it gives you a chance to bring issues to the dealer's attention before delivery. A warranty can help cover eligible repairs, but you may still have to wait for a service appointment.

Why Choose a Certified RV Inspector?

An RV packs a lot into a small space. It has plumbing, propane, batteries, 120-volt electrical circuits, appliances, heating and cooling, and often slide-outs or leveling equipment. A system can appear to work during a quick walkthrough and still perform poorly when tested.

A certified RV inspector is trained to check those systems methodically and document what they find. I use inspection software designed for RVs to record the condition of the roof, exterior, and accessible systems. The result is a written record you can refer to when deciding what to ask the seller about before you buy.

I am certified through the National RV Inspectors Association (NRVIA) which enforce vigorous education, requirements, and code of ethics for its members.

What Happens During an Inspection?

Plan on about five to ten hours on site, depending on the RV's size and features. The on-site inspection takes place in a single calendar day. I take hundreds of photos and select the ones that best show the RV's condition and the findings that matter. A detailed report may run 100 pages or more and include around 80 to 100 photos.

The report lists the most serious findings first, including any life safety concerns. If a finding raises a safety concern, I may recommend having a qualified RV technician evaluate it before you enter, drive, or move the RV.

The inspection documents observed conditions and potential problems. A qualified technician can use those findings to diagnose the cause and recommend repairs. For motorhomes, the inspection covers the RV portion. Arrange a separate mechanical inspection if you want the engine evaluated.

To get an idea of what you'll receive, take a look at the inspection report I prepared on my own Forest River Rockwood MiniLite 2515S for my National RV Inspectors Academy final exam.
